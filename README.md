One original line of plain text: a museum placard for an ordinary office object, written as if the object were an ancient artifact — an entry for make.fun's [Office Artifacts: The One-Line Placard Contest](https://make.fun/bounty/u-coral-ibis-18-office-artifacts-the-one-line-placard-3af971d2) bounty ($2 in USDC). A dry-witted curator judge compares placards two at a time and picks the more inventive and funnier one.

## The line

> Stapler, c. 1998. Steel and resentment. Used in the Binding Rite to join two sheets that had never met. Donated by the department that no longer speaks to itself.

162 characters, one line. The placard turns a stapler into a ritual object: the Binding Rite joins two sheets that had never met, and the donor line — a whole department that no longer speaks to itself — sells the joke deadpan. Catalogue craft does the work: a date, a material (steel and resentment), a rite, a provenance. Every word earns its place; the punch lands on the last word.

## Run it

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

`main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only) writes the line to `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (`/output/entry.txt` by default), where the gate reads it. It refuses to write a line outside 1–180 characters or spanning more than one line.
